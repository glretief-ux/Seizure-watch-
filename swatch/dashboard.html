How to read this map

This map checks the tool itself, not the drug trade. The dashboard only knows about seizures that appear in the news it searches, so it shows where that search is working and where it may be missing things.

Green: seizure reports were found for this country (bigger circle = more reports). Yellow: nothing found, although the country's language is searched. Either nothing was reported or the news was not picked up. Red: none of the country's main languages is searched, so nothing can appear from there. Dark ring: an official customs or police website is searched as well.

Use it to judge gaps. Few or no reports from a country does not mean little smuggling; a yellow or red dot may only mean the tool cannot see it. Adding a language or an official website for those countries is the way to close the gap.

Alerts

Repeat links

The same vessel, container, loading place or cover cargo appearing in more than one separate seizure. These are leads to check, not proof of a link. A vessel or container number recurring is the strongest sign; a big shipping line or a busy port appears in many seizures by chance.

Production chain watch

Precursor chemical seizures and clandestine-laboratory reports, followed by synthetic-drug seizures in the same country. A match on place and timing is a possible link only. Precursor seizures are rarely reported in the news, so labs are included too.

What to look for (indicator cards)

Practical starting points in the style of PCCP risk indicators. They are general guidance compiled from common customs practice and from the reports in this tool. They are not official PCCP or WCO material, so check them against your training and local procedures. An indicator is a reason to look closer, never proof. Use several together, base checks on cargo, documents and route (not on a person's nationality or appearance), and follow your agency's legal powers and safety rules.
